## 1. Selected Architecture and Rationale

After reviewing the two starting sources and the primary research literature they cite, we selected **Approach A (Documented Device)** anchored on the **Stanford Biomechatronics Laboratory untethered ankle exoskeleton prototype** documented by **Slade, Kochenderfer, Delp, and Collins (2022)** in *Nature* ([PMC9556303](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9556303/)), which is the direct subject of the starting NIH Research Matters summary ([NIH, Oct 25, 2022](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/robotic-exoskeleton-helps-people-walk)).

Broader industry taxonomy, clinical rehabilitation context, and cross-architecture comparisons are grounded in the starting review paper by **Siviy, Baker, Quinlivan, Porciuncula, Swaminathan, Awad, and Walsh (2023)** in *Nature Biomedical Engineering* ([PMC11536595](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11536595/)).

### Why this architecture was chosen
1. **Complete mechanical and control documentation:** Slade et al. (2022) provide explicit component specifications, CAD diagrams (Figure 4b, Extended Data Figure 3), mass breakdown, actuator and transmission geometry, sensor placement, sampling frequency, and load-reaction mechanisms.
2. **Coherent lower-limb/ankle-assistance thread:** Using this single untethered ankle architecture prevents combining unrelated components from incompatible devices (such as mixing a soft Bowden-cable exosuit with a rigid full-body weight-bearing frame).
3. **Clear boundary between documented hardware and schematic visualization:** Where internal soft-tissue deformation, biological talocrural axis migration, or time-normalized curves are shown to explain a physical principle, they are explicitly labeled as schematic or qualitative illustrations.

## 3. Separation of Documented Facts, Interpretive Simplifications, and Unresolved Details

### Documented Facts (Directly Established in Primary Sources)
- **System Mass & Power:** The untethered ankle exoskeleton has a worn mass of **1.2 kg per ankle** and is powered by a waist-worn **24 V, 1,300 mAh lithium-polymer battery** weighing **0.3 kg** (providing **36.3 minutes** of continuous operation under maximum assistance at 1.5 m/s) (`SRC-SLADE`, Fig. 4b, Methods).
- **Attachment Interfaces:**
  - *Proximal shank:* Velcro straps pass through slots at the top of the carbon-fiber calf strut and adhere to a strap worn on the shank just below the knee, paired with interchangeable mediolateral calf spacers (`SRC-SLADE`, Extended Data Fig. 3, Methods).
  - *Distal shoe:* A Nike running shoe (0.3 kg) connects to the toe strut via pins extending into a carbon-fiber plate embedded in the sole, and to the heel spur via a rope tied to holes on either side of the spur and routed through a plastic tube embedded in the sole (`SRC-SLADE`, Extended Data Fig. 3, Methods).
- **Actuation & Transmission:** A brushless gearmotor (**CubeMars AK80-9**, 9:1 single-stage planetary gear, 0.5 kg, 18 Nm rated peak motor torque) drives a custom **7075-aluminum drum** (**0.020 m radius**) that winds a **Vectran transmission cable** attached to the tip of the heel spur (maximum lever arm **0.115 m**, providing an additional ~5:1 mechanical ratio and up to **54 Nm peak assistive ankle torque** at 1.5 m/s) (`SRC-SLADE`, Fig. 4c, Methods).
- **Mechanical Load Transfer & Shear Avoidance:** Because the stiff carbon-fiber and aluminum frame incorporates a mechanical ankle joint, the axial downward component of the cable tension between the drum and heel spur is reacted at the exoskeleton pin joint rather than as downward shear on the wearer's skin (`SRC-SLADE`, Methods citing Witte & Collins 2020). Consequently, the calf interface applies primarily a normal force to the shank while upward tension acts at the heel and downward reaction acts at the forefoot plate (`SRC-SLADE`, Methods).
- **Geometric Alignment & Singularity:** The effective heel-spur lever arm decreases as ankle plantarflexion angle increases, reaching a geometric singularity at **55° plantarflexion** so the cable cannot apply hyperextending torque to the ankle (`SRC-SLADE`, Methods).
- **Onboard Sensing & Control Loop:** Portable electronics (0.15 kg total, centered on a **Raspberry Pi 4b** running at **200 Hz**) fuse three sensors (`SRC-SLADE`, Methods):
  1. An **absolute rotary encoder** at the exoskeleton ankle joint measuring ankle angle ($\theta$) and angular velocity ($\dot{\theta}$).
  2. A **pressure-sensing insole** with four sensing regions (heel, fifth metatarsal, first metatarsal, and distal phalanx of the great toe) to detect heel strikes and stride duration ($t_{\text{stride}}$).
  3. A **full Wheatstone bridge of strain gauges** on the superior and inferior surfaces of the heel lever (with a **Futek IAA100** amplifier) that directly measures ankle bending moment independent of cable force or transmission/interface compliance.
- **Actuation Timing & Cable Slack:** Torque profiles are parameterized by peak torque magnitude, rise time, peak time (fixed at **54.6% of the gait cycle** in untethered outdoor trials), and fall time (fixed at **10.0% of the gait cycle**, terminating torque by toe-off around **62% stride**) (`SRC-SLADE`, Extended Data Fig. 1, Methods). During the swing phase (~62% to 100% stride) and Zero Torque mode, the motor drives **slack into the cable** so zero torque and zero linear-feedback damping impede the biological limb (`SRC-SLADE`, Methods).
- **Opportunistic Personalization & Outcomes:** A data-driven logistic regression classifier compares pairs of control laws using 64 features (30 binned ankle angles, 30 binned ankle velocities, 4 torque parameters) collected over **44 continuous steps** (discarding the first 6 steps of rapid human adaptation) across **three walking speed bins** (<1.22 m/s, 1.22-1.38 m/s, >1.38 m/s) (`SRC-SLADE`, Fig. 1, Extended Data Fig. 4, Methods). In $n = 10$ healthy adults walking on a 566-m public sidewalk course, Real-world Optimized assistance increased self-selected speed by **9 ± 4%** and reduced energetic cost of transport by **17 ± 5%** compared with normal shoes (and reduced metabolic energy consumption by **23 ± 8%** on a treadmill at 1.5 m/s) (`SRC-SLADE`, Abstract, Fig. 5g-h).
- **Usability & Clinical Context:** Participants gave the research prototype a System Usability Scale (SUS) score of **72.5** (65th percentile), and 6 of 10 preferred it over normal shoes on the outdoor course, but they were neutral on all-day wear comfort and noted that the long posterior heel spur required caution when descending stairs (`SRC-SLADE`, Main text, Methods, Extended Data Tables 3-5). Across clinical rehabilitation populations (stroke, SCI, MS, CP, TBI), Siviy et al. (2023) document high inter-patient responder variability, minor skin redness/abrasion from strap fit, fall risk requiring supervision, and inconclusive multicenter RCT superiority over conventional physical therapy (`SRC-PMC-REV`, Table 1, "In-clinic validation").

### Interpretive Simplifications (Visual & Pedagogical Choices)
- **Schematic Anatomy:** The tibia, fibula, talus, calcaneus, and metatarsals are rendered as clean 2D vector silhouettes to clarify spatial orientation and joint axes. They do not represent subject-specific CT/MRI bone geometry.
- **Sectional Cutaway (Chapter 2):** The cross-section of the calf cuff, soft-tissue pad, mechanical pin joint, heel rope tube, and carbon sole plate is an illustrative schematic designed to separate physical structure from gold force/torque arrows.
- **Amplified Axis Offset (Chapter 3):** To make the difference between a fixed mechanical pin pivot and the shifting biological talocrural axis visually legible at 1920x1080 and mobile viewports, the instantaneous axis locus and cuff micro-travel are drawn with schematic amplification.
- **Qualitative Timing Curves (Chapter 4):** The synchronized gait-cycle waveforms for biological ankle angle ($\theta_{\text{bio}}$), device assistive torque ($\tau_{\text{exo}}$), and cable tension state are smooth mathematical curves anchored to the documented gait-cycle percentages (0% heel strike, ~54.6% peak torque, ~62% toe-off, 62-100% swing slack). Because we do not plot raw participant CSV traces, numerical vertical axis units are omitted and the graph is explicitly labeled **"Qualitative illustration—not measured data."**

### Unresolved Details and What This Explanation Does Not Establish
- **No Quantified Interface Pressure or Tissue Stress:** Neither starting source nor Slade et al. (2022) reports kPa strap pressure distributions or internal soft-tissue shear/normal stress maps for this prototype. No pressure heatmaps or numerical tissue stress values are shown.
- **No Clinical Efficacy Claims for the Untethered Prototype:** The Stanford untethered prototype was evaluated in 10 healthy young adults (age 24.2 ± 1.8 yr) with prior exoskeleton walking experience. Its findings do not establish therapeutic efficacy for stroke, spinal-cord injury, cerebral palsy, or other gait impairments.
- **No Operating, Fitting, or Medical Instructions:** This work does not establish clinical prescription criteria, strap tightening torques, control parameter settings for individuals, or unsupervised home-use safety.
